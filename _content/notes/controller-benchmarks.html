The question

What evidence is needed before a controller comparison informs an engineering decision? The public ProxMPC benchmark documentation is the starting point for this outline, not a basis for claiming universal controller rankings.

1. Establish what was tested

Record the software revision, controller configuration, plant, host hardware and scenario. In the ProxMPC documentation, kinematic-plant results and the limited Gazebo gate are different kinds of evidence. Neither establishes physical hardware performance.

2. Read more than computation time

Discuss tracking, goal completion, obstacle clearance and collisions alongside per-cycle computation. A lower compute time does not on its own establish better navigation behaviour.

3. Account for repeated runs

Explain why a single run or a single difficult scenario is insufficient for a general conclusion. The completed article should trace reported aggregates back to their experimental conditions and source record.

4. Keep validation boundaries explicit

Distinguish a simulated collision criterion from physical contact, and a simulation comparison from real robot validation. Document unresolved failure cases rather than presenting a controller as a safety layer.

Before this becomes an article

  • Pin the benchmark revision and establish the experiment dates.
  • Select a small set of source-backed examples without reproducing a changing leaderboard.
  • Separate reported observations from interpretation and proposed future tests.
  • Complete author review and record the actual publication date.
